## STORY 1 (proposed LEAD) — AI hacking tool traced in South Korean bank breaches

### 5W1H
- WHO: Shinhan, KB Kookmin, Hana, BNK Busan, Yegaram Savings Bank (data exposures); Woori, NH NongHyup (attempts, no confirmed leak); CrowdStrike (analysis)
- WHAT: Breaches of supporting/peripheral systems; investigators found traces of ARTEX, a Chinese-language open-source autonomous penetration-testing tool driven by LLMs
- WHEN: Late Sep → early Oct 2026 (KST, UTC+9); Korea Times explainer 2026-10-07; CybersecurityNews 2026-10-08
- WHERE: South Korea
- WHY NOW: ARTEX published on GitHub 2026-07-26, weeks before the breaches; agentic AI lowers the skill floor for attacks
- HOW: Shinhan — broker-facing loan-progress inquiry service, brute-forced customer numbers; Kookmin — internal mobile work-support system. Core internet/mobile banking reportedly NOT compromised

### Counter-view
- Prof. Lim Jong-in (Korea Univ.): the actor is unknown; Chinese-language tooling ≠ Chinese state. [1]
- Prof. Hwang Suk-jin: damage limited — entry via weaker peripheral networks, not core banking; but may be a test run for larger attacks. [1]
- CrowdStrike: no link to a known group; financial motive inferred from operator sessions, no proof data was sold. [2]

### Lao angle
- Same attack surface exists in Lao banks (BCEL, LDB, JDB …): broker/agent portals, staff mobile apps, loan-inquiry endpoints that leak customer numbers. No Lao incident reported — do not imply one.
- Actionable: rate-limit + enumeration protection on lookup APIs, MFA on staff mobile tools, audit third-party/peripheral systems, monitor agentic-scanner traffic patterns.
